# Prompt: a comprehensive review of the CalBallot home page, with layout suggestions

**For:** Fable, in Claude Code desktop, on branch `phase0-election-status`.
**Type:** review and proposals only. No code changes and no commits.

---

Igor wants a broad, candid look at CalBallot's **home page**: everything a visitor meets from
the top of `index.html` down. That covers the header and search, the view switcher,
orientation and introduction, the statewide slate, local measures, the stats ribbon, filters,
the Grid/List catalog, the quiz, "Ask AI", the About modal, the footer, and whatever else is
there. He wants suggestions on **layout**: what goes where, what comes first, what to cut or
merge, and how it works on a phone. Be specific and opinionated. Where a call is his (taste,
voice, product direction), give options rather than deciding.

## Context

- **Product.** CalBallot is a free, neutral database of California ballot measures, 1998 on.
  It has about 12,400 records: 14 statewide propositions and 49 local measures (San
  Bernardino and San Mateo) on the November 3, 2026 ballot, plus history.
  - The election has passed. Current measures read "Results pending"; results arrive before
    certification on December 3.
  - Coverage will grow to all 53 counties with measures, about 460 local measures, in coming
    weeks.
  - Read `CLAUDE.md`, especially the "paradigm-as-moat" note: free deterministic facts, plus
    editorial briefings on demand.
- **Hidden right now:** Insights is hidden for renovation. Explore was hidden on the live site
  and is being rebuilt; it shows in the preview below.
- **Prior design work: build on it, don't duplicate it.**
  - `docs/plans/measure_layout_design_proposal.md` is "Direction A", a statewide slate plus a
    county directory. Igor approved it. Its mockups are in `docs/design/measure-layouts/`.
  - `docs/codex/layout_direction_a_review_response.md` is Codex's review of Direction A:
    ready with changes, with a six-chunk build order, result states and routing rules. Your
    suggestions should fit with it or explicitly argue against it.
  - `docs/codex/homepage_introduction_design_prompt.txt` is an earlier, unrun brief about the
    page's introduction and top-of-page design. Treat it as Igor's earlier thinking.
  - `docs/plans/explore_review_20261112.md` is your Explore review. Explore's entry points
    and its relationship to the home page are in scope; Explore's internals are not.
  - The checklist `docs/plans/local_measures_checklist_20261107.md` has the plan's phases:
    results import (P2), the layout work (P3/P3b) and county expansion (P1/P4).
- **Code.** The home page is generated by `scraper/src/website/generator.py`, one large
  f-string with JavaScript inside, so braces are doubled. The page loads a 35 MB
  `measures-data.json`. Line numbers drift; search by element IDs.
- **Preview.** http://127.0.0.1:8917/ serves the current candidate build. It matches the live
  site, plus the rebuilt Explore.

## What to do

1. **Use it.**
   - Visit as four people would:
     - a first-time voter who just heard about a local measure;
     - a returning voter checking results;
     - a journalist;
     - a researcher.
   - Note what each sees first, what they're looking for, and how many scrolls and clicks it
     takes.
   - Do it at 1440, 1024, 768, 390 and 320 px.
   - Measure where key things sit (pixels from the top), and the page's total height at each
     width.
   - Save screenshots to `scraper/data/exports/homepage_review_20261113/` (gitignored). Use
     Playwright or a local browser against `127.0.0.1:8917` only.
2. **Read the code** enough to understand each section, what it depends on, and what's dead
   or duplicated: carousels, the featured block, the welcome intro, the stats ribbon, the
   quiz, "Ask AI".
3. **Audit:**
   - **Information architecture:** sections, their order and their jobs; duplication and
     dead ends; what belongs on the home page versus a dedicated page.
   - **First screen:** what a visitor understands in five seconds, at desktop and on a phone.
   - **The post-election moment:** what the page should lead with now, in the results
     period, and later as counties are added.
   - **Navigation:** the header, "Jump to", the view switcher, filters, and Back.
   - **Visual design:** hierarchy, density, typography, consistency, and use of color.
   - **Phones:** length, order and tap targets.
   - **Accessibility:** headings outline, landmarks, focus order, and contrast.
   - **Performance as the reader feels it:** time to first useful content with a 35 MB
     catalog, and what could load later.
   - **Honesty:** anything that claims more than the data supports, or promises features
     that are hidden or missing.
4. **Propose.**
   - **Ranked suggestions** in three tiers: quick wins, medium, and ambitious restructures.
     For each, give what changes, why, dependencies (data, Direction A chunks, county
     expansion), effort, risk, and how a reviewer would verify it.
   - **Two or three page concepts.** Give each an ASCII wireframe for desktop and phone, and
     say who it serves best and how it sequences with Direction A.
   - **A recommended page order** for now, the results period, and for when all 53 counties
     are loaded.
   - **What not to do**, and why.
   - **A first chunk:** scope, files touched, acceptance checks, and a preservation gate
     (what must stay byte-identical, e.g. every measure page and `measures-data.json`).
